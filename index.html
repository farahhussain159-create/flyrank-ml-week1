Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring: Predicting Declining Content

FlyRank ML Internship Capstone — Hussain

Abstract

This paper asks which pages a content team should prioritize for refresh, using a classifier that flags content likely to be declining in search performance. A Random Forest model was trained on FlyRank's warehouse data to predict a binary "declining" label. The model's top signals — impressions over the last 90 days, average search position, and content age — align with intuitive decline patterns. Out of the evaluation set, the model produced 1796 false positives and 812 false negatives, giving the content team a starting point for triage rather than a fully automated decision.

1. Introduction: The Question

Decision this work improves: which content pages to prioritize for refresh, out of a large catalog, when the team cannot review everything manually.

Who acts on it: the content/SEO team, who would refresh, rewrite, or otherwise update pages flagged as high-risk of decline.

Cost of a wrong call: a false positive wastes the team's limited refresh capacity on a page that was not actually declining. A false negative lets a genuinely declining page go unnoticed, continuing to lose visibility and traffic.

2. Data

The dataset comes from the FlyRank Warehouse (content-level search performance data). Key fields used: impressions_90d, avg_position, content_age_days, char_count, word_count, days_since_last_update, search_volume, ctr, and the target label is_declining_label.

3. Methodology

The task is framed as binary classification: predict whether a piece of content is declining. Data was split using GroupShuffleSplit by client_id to prevent leakage across clients. A Random Forest classifier was trained and compared against a simpler rule-based baseline (freshness + volume weighting).

A validation audit in an earlier phase of this work found that a naive (non-grouped) split overstated performance — Precision@50 was 0.840 under a naive split vs. 0.580 under the honest, grouped split. All results reported here use the grouped, leakage-safe split.

On the evaluation set, the model produced 1796 false positives (flagged as declining but weren't) and 812 false negatives (missed a real decline). The three dominant features — impressions, position, and content age — together explain most of the model's decisions: content that is losing visibility and ranking poorly as it ages carries the clearest signal of decline.

5. Limitations

This model is decision-support, not causal proof. It does not claim to know why Google ranks a page lower, only that certain observed, directional patterns correlate with a page later being labeled as declining. It should not be used to make unilateral refresh decisions without human review, especially given the false positive/negative rates above.

6. Recommendations

  • Use the model's rf_proba score to rank pages for manual review, not as an auto-refresh trigger.
  • Prioritize review of pages with high impressions_90d decline combined with worsening avg_position.
  • Re-validate the model periodically since false negative pages (missed declines) risk going unnoticed longest.
